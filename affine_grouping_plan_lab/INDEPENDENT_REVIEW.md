# Independent review of complete XOR3 occurrence grouping

Verdict: clean for the frozen planner, theorem and saved original requests. The proposal is an occurrence order, not a substitute XOR factor or a SAT answer. I read the source and theorem, independently checked the complete four-assignment block condition and original-ID partition, and replayed the saved gzip artifacts against their external original inputs. No producer or frozen solver change was needed.

A three-distinct-variable clause is false on exactly one local assignment. Four distinct forbidden words of the same parity exhaust that entire four-word parity class. The independent review enumerates each claimed clause's eight local assignments and then checks the conjunction's eight values against the stated parity. It also requires that each claimed block own the entire eligible original support bucket, so a duplicated eligible occurrence cannot be silently excluded from a favorable four-clause subset.

Every grouped occurrence ID is distinct and belongs to its original clause. All other IDs must be the original-order complement, and concatenation must be a full permutation of the original occurrence range. Literal signs, empty clauses, duplicate clauses, unused original variables and all ungrouped constraints therefore remain in the solver's original request. The checker does not need to certify that every discoverable block was selected to prove permutation safety. Its conservative complete-bucket rule and canonical ordering are explicitly checked for the blocks it does claim.

The review's separate occurrence checker uses explicit local truth evaluation and full original-ID coverage. It does not invoke the planner's discovery procedure. The source `verify_plan` also succeeds with discovery disabled. Adversarial checks cover missing/repeated IDs, Boolean metadata, wrong parity, changed bindings, rehashed omitted original constraints, and a rehashed subset of a duplicated bucket. Planning and verification cap controls return UNKNOWN with no accepted order.

For every saved case, the review checks its gzip hash, decompresses the complete proof, compares n, clauses, occurrence order, reference index, expected count and solver caps with the frozen original dataset, and replays the planner and solver certificate. The 22 structural stress references are checked again against their source constructions. All 58 earlier originals remain separately identified. The original clause lists are not normalized or replaced in this comparison. The eight grouped/shuffled parity pairs produce identical canonical ordered clause lists and consistent completed statuses/counts after planning.

Every saved stopping status is preserved. A failed solver cap is not replaced by an analytic reference answer. Completed certificates are inspected to determine whether a proposed four-clause group actually forms one structural subtree; contiguity alone is not treated as proof of that alignment. Planner work, plan verification, solver work and solver replay are accounted separately and checked against the recorded pipeline totals. These are logical counters, not precise runtime or byte-memory claims; planning adds work outside the unchanged solver-only limits.

Reproduce with `python INDEPENDENT_REVIEW.py`. `INDEPENDENT_REVIEW.json` contains all 80 artifact hashes, the recorded source hashes, independent occurrence and truth-pattern totals, fresh verification counters, complete status summaries, canonical-pair comparisons and adversarial-check counts. Frozen sources, gzip proofs and result summaries are checked unchanged after the run. This is a finite original-instance review, not a theorem that grouping finds an optimal decomposition or makes arbitrary SAT tractable.
