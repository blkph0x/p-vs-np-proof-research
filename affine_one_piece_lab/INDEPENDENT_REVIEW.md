The result types are used correctly. EXACT supplies a canonical zero or a checked one-piece equality. UNSUPPORTED supplies a mathematical obstruction to this one-piece representation class, rather than a failed heuristic. UNKNOWN carries neither a message nor a certificate. It does not mean the function needs multiple pieces. All original occurrences, including zeros and cancellations, remain externally bound.

The norm checker reconstructs every nonzero original operand and the negative candidate if present, requires every diagonal and unordered off-diagonal pair exactly once, derives the doubled coefficients itself, and verifies each compact block contraction against those derived inputs and restrictions. It recalculates the rational total. The frozen block verifier permits equivalent noncanonical constant phases, so evaluating the verified closed message's scalar together with its constant sign is necessary and correctly implemented. Its message parser excludes inconsistent nonzero supports; hence no hidden zero-support condition is ignored by the closed evaluator.

The fast candidate from the hull of all input supports is positive-only. It is accepted solely after a complete zero difference norm. A zero anchor, failed magnitude sample or positive difference norm on that enclosing hull always leads to actual-support discovery, or UNKNOWN on exhaustion. The independent review includes explicit cap interruptions immediately after failed fast candidates; neither may become a false UNSUPPORTED result. Overlapping cancellation can and does reduce the true support hull.

Actual-support discovery searches canonical free coordinates of the enclosing input hull. It need not include those hull equations in every norm restriction because the original sum already vanishes outside that enclosing hull. The negative certificate establishes the true hull independently: every listed point is nonzero and lies in the claimed hull, the point differences are independent and span its dimension, and every violated defining halfspace has a checked zero norm. These facts prove both inclusions needed for exact support-hull equality without rerunning producer search.

The checker then verifies the canonical anchor and required original sample values. A zero anchor contradicts full affine support for a nonzero piece; unequal magnitudes contradict its constant magnitude. Otherwise the low-weight samples force the unique quadratic candidate. A checked positive difference norm excludes that candidate and therefore every one-piece representation. Positive equality certificates intentionally omit hull discovery: a full equality norm already proves the represented function. Omitted discovery traces are not omitted producer work; producer query and contraction counters retain those attempts.

The independent tiny oracle evaluates the original sum at every retained assignment, builds its actual support by direct inspection, tests affine closure, and computes the full Boolean-Mobius sign polynomial. It uses neither the producer's hull search nor its low-weight candidate fitting. Inputs cover every two-bit {-1,0,1} truth table, every three-bit sign table, sparse/permuted coordinates, seeded signed rational sums and named controls. These include the nonzero-support hull with zero canonical origin, full-support cubic signs, overlap cancellation to a halfspace, cancellation of an outlying input support, zero cancellation and a shifted support requiring outside-halfspace proofs.

The script replays every saved author certificate and compares all saved bounded original truth values with the separate oracle. For wide positive cases it additionally assembles an ordered-pair whole-sum norm through the previously frozen independent review tool, rather than trusting the recognizer's triangular pair assembly. Both layers use the already audited block contraction primitive; this is independence of the recognition/norm assembly, not a claim of two unrelated arithmetic foundations.
