# Independent review of complete one-piece recognition

Verdict: the theorem in `AFFINE_ONE_PIECE_RECOGNITION_DESIGN.md` is sound for the stated explicit list of rational, real-valued affine quadratic pieces. I found no mathematical correction. The review binds the exact design SHA-256 `157107c1edb28d7f5f312b1b1580cfcacde9f654c6df59b5b970b26ec8b36b3a`; the adjacent script refuses a different draft.

Every affine-restricted squared norm expands into K diagonal and K(K−1)/2 doubled off-diagonal pair contractions. Pair supports intersect, phases add over GF(2), and rational coefficients multiply. Although individual contractions can be negative, the completed norm is a sum of real squares, so it vanishes exactly when the function vanishes on that restriction. The existing closed affine quadratic marginalization theorem supplies the polynomial compact contraction. The argument would not justify an ordinary square for complex coefficients; the design explicitly uses real rational coefficients.

Positive restricted norm permits coordinate prefix search because the two coordinate halves partition the current restriction. A support point found outside the current affine hull strictly raises its direction rank. At termination, the zero norms on every violated defining row show that no nonzero support lies outside that hull. Conversely, the explicitly witnessed nonzero points span it. Thus the constructed hull is exactly the affine hull of the true support, including cancellation among overlapping input pieces. There are at most m strict rank increases; repeated row scans and prefix searches use O(m²) norm queries, with polynomial linear algebra overhead.

The corrected canonical-anchor rule is necessary. A hull origin need not be one of the discovered nonzero points. A nonzero one-piece function must be nonzero everywhere on its affine support, so a zero value at that origin rejects the one-piece class. Otherwise the value there fixes the normalized rational coefficient. Samples at free-coordinate weights one and two determine every coefficient of the unique possible quadratic sign polynomial; unequal magnitudes immediately reject it. The final exact squared norm of f−g proves pointwise equality when zero. When positive, no alternative one-piece candidate remains: its support would have to be this same hull, and its coefficient and phase would have to match the samples. Zero f is handled separately.

The bit-cost argument is also sound. If input denominators have total bit length B, their product is a common denominator with O(B) bits; squared norms need its square. A finite sum over 2^m points contributes at most O(m) further magnitude bits beyond the rational input and log K bounds. Pair contractions and exact arithmetic consequently have polynomial bit complexity in explicit m and the full input encoding. This is not a claim polynomial in log m for a compressed enormous unused coordinate universe. The final piece and its quadratic phase have polynomial description length. Exact cap exhaustion must still be UNKNOWN, not a negative representation decision.

The design correctly excludes the circular shortcut from a succinct CNF product. Expanding that product can create exponentially many pieces, and its unexpanded squared norm is the satisfying-assignment count itself. One-piece recognition from an already explicit sum does not bound that expansion, solve minimum-piece decomposition, guarantee cheap repeated recognition at solver nodes, or settle P versus NP. Recognition failure is confined to this representation class.

`AFFINE_ONE_PIECE_RECOGNITION_REVIEW.py` is only a bounded executable review: dimensions are at most five, and it deliberately enumerates at most 32 assignments. It independently expands every queried pair norm and compares it to direct squares. It reconstructs the support hull through those norm queries, compares it with direct support closure, and compares the sampled candidate/final norm with full Boolean-Mobius phase classification. It imports no production recognizer or marginalizer.

The required controls are explicit: the sum of singleton pins at 01,10,11 rejects because its full hull has zero canonical origin; the full-support cubic sign on three bits passes all low-weight samples but has difference norm four against their constant candidate; and `1_full−1_{x0=0}` is accepted as the remaining halfspace despite overlap and cancellation. Exact zero cancellation is also included. Exhaustive two-bit {-1,0,1} tables, all three-bit sign tables, and seeded rational sums supplement these controls. The adjacent JSON saves all original pieces, classifications, hull witnesses, query/pair counts and hashes. These finite checks corroborate rather than replace the all-size proof.

Reproduce with `python AFFINE_ONE_PIECE_RECOGNITION_REVIEW.py`. No production implementation, solver integration or frozen source was changed.
