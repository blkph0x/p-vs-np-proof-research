The unit verifier is sound: each step cites a real original clause whose previously assigned literals are all false and whose only remaining distinct literal is the one forced. It rejects an already assigned variable, a satisfied clause, a non-unit residual, malformed exact integers and invalid indices. The final cited clause must have every literal assigned false. An original empty clause is consequently a valid immediate contradiction. Duplicate literal occurrences do not invalidate a semantic unit clause; a tautology cannot force a literal or become a conflict. A unit contradiction may use only a subset of original clauses, which is sufficient for UNSAT and does not require the complete coverage condition needed for counting certificates.
